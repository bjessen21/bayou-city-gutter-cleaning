Clean before hurricane season starts

Hurricane season runs June through November. The best time to clean gutters is May: after the spring oak shed and pine pollen, and before the first tropical system. Clean gutters give a hard rain somewhere to go. Packed gutters turn a tropical downpour into sheet overflow at your foundation and siding.

Our Houston gutter cleaning schedule shows how a May visit fits with the rest of the year.

Pre-hurricane-season gutter checklist

This is the list we work through on a May or early June visit.

  1. Empty every run to the bottom of the trough. Live oak leaves and catkins from the February–April shed break down into a dense mat that holds water. Skimming the top layer off doesn't fix it.
  2. Flush each downspout and confirm flow at the outlet. A downspout that takes a garden hose can still choke in a tropical downpour if the elbow is half packed.
  3. Check that the extensions and splash blocks are in place. Water dumped at the slab is how Houston's expansive clay ends up swelling unevenly against a foundation. See gutters and foundation problems.
  4. Tighten loose hangers and look at the slope. A sagging section holds standing water. That water adds weight right when wind loads are highest.
  5. Look at the fascia behind the gutter. Soft or stained wood means water has already been getting behind the gutter.
  6. Clear the roof valleys and the edge above the gutter. Pine needles in the Woodlands, Kingwood and other wooded lots slide off the roof in the first heavy rain and refill a clean gutter.
  7. Photograph the gutters once they're clean. A dated "before" photo makes it easier to show storm damage later.

Why Houston gutters struggle in tropical rain

Houston storms rarely ruin gutters with wind alone. The usual problem is volume. Tropical systems and stalled fronts can drop several inches of rain in a few hours, and a standard 5-inch K-style gutter with partly blocked downspouts can't move that much water. It overflows along the whole run instead of at one corner.

The ground makes it worse. Much of the city sits on flat, slow-draining clay, and neighborhoods along Brays, Buffalo and White Oak bayous (Meyerland, Braeswood, the Heights, Memorial) already have water with nowhere to go during a big rain. Gutter water dumped at the foundation adds to it. Harvey in 2017 and Beryl in 2024 both showed how fast that ground saturates.

What pre-season and storm cleanup cost

A pre-season cleaning is priced like a regular cleaning. On our Houston gutter cleaning cost page, the ranges run about $155–$260 for a one-story home and $190–$370 for a two-story home. Those ranges assume normal debris, walkable access and downspouts that flush clear.

Post-storm cleanup usually costs more than that range, because the time goes into wet debris, broken branches and downspouts packed solid. We price it once we've seen the house. If a section needs re-hanging, that's quoted separately as gutter repair, so you can decide on it apart from the cleaning.

Storm season gutter cleanup

After a storm: what we do

  1. Clear storm debris

    Branches, leaf mass, shingle pieces and wet pine needles come out of gutters and off the roof edge where it is safe.

  2. Check the downspouts

    Storm debris loves downspouts. We flush and confirm flow.

  3. Look for damage

    Bent or torn-away sections, loose hangers, and damaged fascia are noted.

  4. Report clearly

    You get a plain list of what we found so you can decide about repairs or an insurance claim.

What to look for after a Houston storm

  • Gutters pulled away from the fascia or hanging lower on one end
  • Dents or crushed sections from falling limbs
  • Roof granules in the gutter (a gritty dark sand), which may point to roof damage
  • Downspouts knocked loose or extensions moved out of place
  • Water stains under the eave or behind the gutter
Stay off the ladder if power lines are down or the roof is wet. After a storm, wait until it is safe, and do not clean gutters near damaged lines.

If you plan to file a claim, take photos of damage before anything is cleaned or moved, and ask your insurer what documentation they need. We are not an insurance adjuster and do not give claims advice.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

When should I clean gutters before hurricane season?

May is ideal in Houston. It comes after the spring shed of oak leaves and pollen and before the June 1 start of hurricane season.

Should I clean gutters right before a tropical storm?

Only if it is safe and there is time. A day or two ahead is fine. Never climb a ladder in wind or rain.

Can clogged gutters make storm damage worse?

Yes. Overflow can soak fascia, soffits and the ground at your foundation. Clear gutters give a hard rain a route away from the house.

Is it too late to clean gutters in October for hurricane season?

No. Hurricane season runs through November 30, and October still brings tropical systems and heavy fronts to the Gulf Coast. A fall cleaning also clears early leaf drop before the winter rains.

How much does storm gutter cleanup cost in Houston?

A routine cleaning runs about $155 to $260 for one story and $190 to $370 for two stories. After a storm, the price is usually higher because of wet debris and blocked downspouts, so it is quoted after seeing the home. Any repair is quoted separately.

Do gutter guards mean I can skip the pre-season cleaning?

Not entirely. Gutter guards keep most leaves out, but oak catkins, pollen and roof grit still build up on and under the screens. A quick check and rinse before June keeps them from shedding water over the edge in a downpour.
