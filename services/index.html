What Professional Gutter Cleaning Includes in Houston

People search for this work under a lot of names: rain gutter cleaning, roof gutter cleaning, gutter maintenance, professional gutter cleaning. In Houston they almost always describe the same visit, and the scope should be the same whatever it is called. Here is what a complete cleaning covers, and when one of the specialty pages above is the better fit.

Rain gutter and roof gutter cleaning

A professional cleaning should cover every gutter run on the house, not only the front elevation. Debris comes out by hand or vacuum, the roof edge and valleys are cleared where they can be reached safely, and the ground is left clean. On Houston roofs the debris is usually a mix: live oak leaves and catkins in spring, loblolly pine needles all year, pecan and sweetgum in the fall, and asphalt shingle grit that settles in the bottom of the trough like wet sand. That grit is easy to miss and adds weight that makes gutters sag over time.

Tall homes and narrow townhome lots in Montrose and the Heights need different ladder and pole work, covered on the two-story gutter cleaning page. Lots under big canopies in Memorial or Garden Oaks fill faster and are covered under gutter cleaning for oak, pine and pecan lots.

Downspouts are part of the job

A gutter only works if its downspout drains, so every cleaning should end with water run through each downspout to prove it flows. This matters more in Houston than in most cities. Much of the metro sits on expansive clay that swells when wet and shrinks when dry, and roof water that dumps at one corner of a slab foundation is exactly what that soil handles worst. A clogged elbow or a buried drain line packed with silt can undo a clean gutter. When the problem is underground or at the outlet, downspout cleaning covers that work.

Gutter maintenance between cleanings

For most Houston homes, gutter maintenance is mostly about timing. Two cleanings a year handle the majority of houses: May, after the spring oak shed and before hurricane season starts on June 1, and December, after the fall leaf and needle drop. Lots under heavy pines, pecans or sweetgums often add a September or October visit. The Houston gutter cleaning schedule lays this out month by month.

Between visits, watch for water spilling over the front edge during a heavy rain, dark streaks on the outside of the gutter, and runs that pull away from the fascia. Overflow means a cleaning. A gutter that leaks at a seam or sags between hangers is a repair, not a cleaning problem; see gutter repair in Houston.

Common questions

Gutter cleaning service questions

What is the difference between rain gutter cleaning and roof gutter cleaning?

In practice, nothing. Both mean clearing the gutters that run along the roof edge and the downspouts that drain them. Some companies use the terms to describe the same visit, so compare quotes by what is included, not by the name.

What does professional gutter cleaning include?

A complete cleaning clears every gutter run on the house, clears the roof edge and valleys where they can be reached safely, flushes each downspout with water to confirm it drains, and leaves the ground clean. You should also get a plain list of anything that looks wrong, such as a sagging run or a leaking seam.

How much does professional gutter cleaning cost in Houston?

Most Houston homes run about $155 to $370 for a standard cleaning. Single-story homes are typically $155 to $260, two-story homes $190 to $370 and three-story homes $220 to $460. Gutter length, height, roof pitch and the amount of debris set the final price.

What gutter maintenance can I do myself between cleanings?

Most of it can be done from the ground. Watch the gutters during a heavy rain for water spilling over the front edge, keep downspout outlets and extensions clear and attached, pick up limbs after storms, and look for gutters pulling away from the fascia. Overflow in a normal rain usually means it is time for a cleaning.
