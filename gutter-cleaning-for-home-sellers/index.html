Why gutters matter at listing time

Buyers notice exterior details before they step inside. Overflowing gutters, plants growing from the eaves, dark streaks on the fascia and mud lines on siding all suggest deferred maintenance, and buyers ask what else was deferred.

Houston buyers are also more sensitive to water than most. After years of heavy storms and flood headlines, an agent or inspector who sees a clogged downspout or pooling water at the slab will note it. Texas home inspectors typically report on the roof drainage system, including gutters and downspouts, so a clog or a sag can end up in the report that goes to the buyer’s lender and insurer.

Tips for listing agents

  • Book gutter work before photos. Debris and streaks show on wide-angle exterior shots.
  • Have the downspouts flushed. A buyer who runs a hose or sees water pooling can quickly form an opinion.
  • Cheap fixes first. Reseating a loose hanger or resealing a corner costs a fraction of what a buyer will ask off.
  • Vacant listings. If the house is empty, gutters are one of the first things to slip. Add a visit to the between-showings routine, especially in the wet season.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to clean gutters before selling my house?

It is not required, but it is inexpensive and buyers and inspectors notice gutters. Clean gutters and clear downspouts remove an easy objection.

Can you turn around gutter cleaning quickly before photos?

We try to. Call with your photo date and we will tell you what is possible.

Will the inspector really check gutters?

Inspectors typically look at the roof and drainage system, including gutters and downspouts, and note visible problems like clogs, sags and poor discharge.
