Why gutter guards clog in Houston

Every guard style has a weak spot, and Houston trees find it:

  • Live oak catkins and pollen (spring). Long oak flower strands and fine pollen film can bridge over fine micro-mesh. Rain then runs off the edge instead of into the gutter.
  • Pine needles (fall peak, all year). Needles can slip through wide screen openings into the trough, and on flat guards they lie across the surface and mat.
  • Sweetgum balls and pecan husks. Too big for the gutter, but they pile on top of a guard and hold water and leaves against it.
  • Shingle granules. On newer roofs, loose granules wash into the gutter and settle under the guard into a gritty sludge.
  • Moss and algae. Houston’s humidity grows a green film on shaded guards that catches more debris.

Gutters clog with or without guards

How we clean gutter guards

  1. Look first

    We identify the guard type and whether it is clipped, screwed or slid under the shingles.

  2. Brush and blow the surface

    Surface debris comes off first so it does not fall into the trough.

  3. Lift or remove where needed

    If the trough underneath is packed, guards come up, the gutter is cleaned by hand, and the guards go back.

  4. Flush the system

    We run water through the gutter and downspouts and confirm it drains freely.

  5. Tell you what we found

    If a guard style is not working on your trees, we say so and explain why.

Taking guards off and putting them back is normally an added charge, and the price depends on the style. See typical numbers in our cost guide.

Signs your gutter guards need cleaning

  • Water running over the front of the gutter during a hard rain, even though the gutter is “guarded”
  • Green or brown film on top of the guards
  • Plants growing out of the gutter edge or roof line
  • Mud or stained streaks on the fascia and siding behind the guard
  • A gutter that hangs lower than it used to, from weight of packed debris
Be wary of pressure-washing guards from the ground. It can push debris deeper, damage fasteners and blast granules off shingles. Careful brushing and flushing are better.
Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Do gutter guards mean I never have to clean gutters?

No. Guards reduce how often you need to clean, but oak pollen, pine needles, moss and shingle granules still build up on and under most guards in Houston.

How often should guards be cleaned?

Most Houston homes with guards do well with an annual cleaning, and heavily treed lots often need two. See our cleaning schedule for timing.

Can you tell me whether my guards are worth keeping?

Yes. If a guard type fights your tree species, we will tell you. Our guide to gutter guards in Houston walks through the tradeoffs.
