HOA gutter cleaning services across Houston's community types

Houston associations don't all have the same gutter problem. Who is responsible, how tall the buildings are and what is growing overhead change the scope from one community to the next.

Townhome rows inside the Loop

The three-story townhome rows in Midtown, Montrose, the Heights, Rice Military and EaDo share long gutter runs across several units, often with a rooftop deck or parapet in the way and a narrow shared drive below. One clogged outlet backs water up over the units next to it. These buildings need the same tall-roof methods as our two- and three-story gutter cleaning, and the whole row should be cleaned on the same day so one unit's debris doesn't wash into the next.

Master-planned communities in the suburbs

In Cinco Ranch, First Colony and The Woodlands, homeowners usually own their own gutters, so the association's list is shorter: clubhouses, pool houses, amenity centers, gatehouses and pavilions. These buildings often sit under mature live oaks or, in The Woodlands, loblolly pines, which drop needles almost all year and pack into downspouts.

Condo and garden-style associations

Where the association maintains every exterior, the board is cleaning dozens of buildings at once. Many older Houston complexes tie downspouts into buried drain lines that run out to the curb or a courtyard drain. When those lines silt up, cleaning the gutters alone won't stop the overflow. Our downspout cleaning covers flushing those lines.

Who is responsible: the HOA or the owner?

Your declaration or CC&Rs decide it, not the property manager's habit. Look for the section on exterior or roof maintenance. If the association maintains roofs, gutters almost always come with them. If owners maintain their own exteriors, the HOA usually covers only common buildings. Some associations clean every unit on one contract anyway, because it keeps every unit on the same schedule and avoids one clogged unit wetting its neighbors.

What property managers usually ask about

  • Consistent timing. The same window each year keeps the wet-season risk low and the budget predictable.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How often should an HOA schedule gutter cleaning in Houston?

Twice a year works for most Houston communities: May, before hurricane season, and December, after fall leaf and needle drop. Treed communities may need a third visit.

Do you clean multiple buildings in one visit?

Yes. We group buildings by location to keep visits efficient.

Can you send a report after each visit?

Yes. We can note the buildings cleaned and any repairs we recommend.

Is gutter cleaning the HOA's responsibility or the homeowner's in Houston?

It depends on your declaration or CC&Rs. If the association maintains roofs and exteriors, gutters are normally included. If owners maintain their own exteriors, the HOA usually only covers common buildings like clubhouses and pool houses.

How much does HOA gutter cleaning cost in Houston?

Planning ranges are about $155 to $260 per single-story building, $190 to $370 per two-story building or townhome unit, and $220 to $460 per three-story building, per visit. Heavy debris, buried drain lines and access limits change the final per-building price.

Do three-story townhomes cost more to clean?

Usually, yes. Three-story rows need taller ladders or pole and vacuum equipment, extra care around rooftop decks and parapets, and more time per unit, so they sit at the top of the range.
