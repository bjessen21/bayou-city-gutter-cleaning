What makes a Houston gutter cleaning cost more

Height and access. Three stories, tall foundations, and tight side yards between Heights townhomes slow the work and add risk.
  • Steep or slick roofs. Steep pitches commonly add about 15 percent. Tile and slate roofs cost more because crews should not walk them.
  • Heavy debris. Gutters packed with a full season of pine needles, sweetgum balls and pecan husks take longer than gutters with a light layer of leaves.
  • Overflow at one spot. A clogged corner or valley sends a heavy stream over the gutter edge in the same place every storm.
  • Gutter guards. Taking guards off and putting them back is usually an added fee. See gutter guard cleaning.
  • Clogged downspouts. Blocked or buried drain lines take extra time. See downspout cleaning.
  • Repairs found along the way. Sagging sections, failed sealant and loose hangers are separate from cleaning. Gutter repair typically runs about $240 to $480. See gutter repair.

    • How to compare Houston gutter cleaning quotes

    The cheapest number is not always the cheapest job. Ask every company the same four questions:

    1. Is the downspout included? Cleaning the trough but leaving a clogged downspout means water still backs up on the first hard rain.
    Do you flush the system with water? Flushing is how you find slow drains and leaking seams. A leaf-scoop only is half a job.
  • What happens on tile, slate or copper? The right answer is ladder work, poles or vacuum tools, not walking on fragile roofing.
  • Is the price for the whole house? Some ads quote a starting price that only covers a small single-story home.
    • Be cautious about a price that is far below every other quote. Very low prices are often a starting number that grows once someone is on site.

    Houston averages close to 50 inches of rain a year on about 104 days, and the wet season runs from spring through early fall.
